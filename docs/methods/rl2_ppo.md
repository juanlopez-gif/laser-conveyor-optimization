# PPO baseline on the token policy (0.7.29)

Code: `src/rl2/ppo.py` (trainer), `experiments/rl2_ppo.py` (CLI),
`experiments/rl2_ppo_eval.py` (paired evaluation), `experiments/rl2_ppo_cost.py`,
`experiments/rl2_ppo_anomaly.py`, `experiments/rl2_ppo_latency.py` (§3). Tests: `tests/test_rl2_ppo.py`.
Representation, network, training distribution and expert iteration:
[rl2.md](rl2.md) §2, §3, §6.1, §6.3; the frozen network `models/rl2_gen_s_b.pt`:
rl2.md §8.8. The old PPO (0.5–0.6, another representation): [rl.md](rl.md).

**Question.** What does learning from the search teacher (expert iteration) add
over learning directly from the reward, with the same tokens, the same network, the
same action mask, the same simulator and the same cell distribution? The old PPO of
0.5–0.6 cannot answer it: it used 87 hand-made features and belt-head actions, and
fine-tuned a cloned policy. Here PPO trains the **same** `DispatchNet` (size S, 0.88 M
parameters) from scratch on the **same** training distribution, and is scored on the
**same** validation realizations as the expert-iteration chain.

## 1. Protocol (frozen 2026-11-23, before the full runs)

Section 2 (smoke test) was run before this section was frozen; nothing below was
changed after seeing the results of §3.

### 1.1 Decision process

* **State and decision.** A decision is a state of the exact simulator (`fastsim`)
  with ≥ 2 candidate parts. Decisions with a single candidate are taken without the
  network and are not in the batches, as in `NetPolicy` and in expert iteration.
* **Action.** A candidate part. The policy is the masked categorical
  softmax(logits) of the pointer head; non-candidates and padding get −∞ in
  `DispatchNet.forward` (unchanged), so they have probability exactly 0 (tested).
* **Reward.** Let C(t) = WIP integral + λ_N N_B + λ_T T_B be the penalized objective
  accumulated up to time t, read from `FastSim.cost_so_far()` (N_B is the
  simulator's blocking-event counter, as in the cost-to-go of expert iteration). The
  reward of decision k is r_k = −(C(t_{k+1}) − C(t_k)) where t_{k+1} is the next
  decision with ≥ 2 candidates, or the end of the drained episode for the last one.
  Forced decisions between two network decisions are inside that interval (a
  semi-Markov step).
* **Return.** γ = 1 (finite drained episodes, rl.md §1): the return from decision k is
  −(C(end) − C(t_k)), i.e. **minus the cost-to-go** used as value target by expert
  iteration (`play_episode` in `rl2/expert_iteration.py`). The value head therefore
  keeps its meaning (normalized cost-to-go) and its `RunningScale`; a PPO checkpoint
  is a drop-in `DispatchNet`.
* **Episodes.** `sample_config(seed)` of rl2.md §6.1 unchanged (real / heavy /
  general cells, design mode and `none`, observation levels, bursty arrivals, slow
  heads), 300 s of arrivals then drained. A bursty cell may get no arrival in the
  horizon (1 episode in 4 000 in the smoke test): it has no decision and no sample,
  but counts in the budget.

### 1.2 Advantages and targets

* **GAE in cost units.** For one episode with decisions k = 0..K−1, cost increments
  c_k = C(t_{k+1}) − C(t_k) and predicted cost-to-go V_k (the value head
  denormalized with the running scale the acting network was trained with, V_K = 0):
  δ_k = c_k + V_{k+1} − V_k, A^c_k = Σ_{l≥0} λ^l δ_{k+l}, with **λ = 0.95**, γ = 1.
* **Policy advantage:** −A^c_k, normalized per batch (mean 0, std 1 over all the
  decisions of the batch).
* **Value target:** the GAE return A^c_k + V_k, normalized by the running scale.
  The scale (`RunningScale`: running mean and std over all targets seen so far, as
  in expert iteration) is updated with the Monte-Carlo cost-to-go C(end) − C(t_k) of
  every new batch **before** the targets of that batch are normalized and after the
  advantages were computed. It is saved with every checkpoint.

### 1.3 PPO update (hyperparameters)

| item | value |
|---|---|
| surrogate | clipped, ε = 0.2; no value clipping; no KL stop |
| epochs per update | 4 |
| minibatch | 256 decisions, reshuffled every epoch |
| entropy coefficient | 0.01 (entropy of the masked categorical) |
| value coefficient | 0.5 (MSE on normalized targets) |
| optimizer | AdamW, lr 3 × 10⁻⁴ constant, weight decay 10⁻⁴ (as expert iteration), one optimizer for the whole run |
| gradient clipping | global norm 1 |
| network | `DispatchNet` size S, random initialization (torch seed = PPO seed) |
| batch | all decisions of 1 000 episodes generated by the current network with **sampled** actions, on CPU workers (40 processes, 1 torch thread each); the update on the GPU (RTX 3080) |

### 1.5 Validation and evaluation

* **Validation groups** (`VAL_GROUPS`, `val_config`, design mode, rl2.md §6.1 and
  §8.8): `real` (seeds 2 000–2 049, `when_idle`), `heavy` (2 000–2 049, `when_idle`),
  `general` (100 cells, seeds 2 500–2 599, mode drawn), `real_burst` (2 000–2 049,
  `when_idle`, bursty arrivals). Per group the mean paired ratio of penalized flow
  time to `nearest_job` on the same realization, minus 1; the **score** is the mean
  of the four groups (the expert-iteration score; `rl2_gen_s_b` has −3.82 %).
* **Evaluation** (`experiments/rl2_ppo_eval.py`), on the same realizations, paired
  per realization, greedy policies, exact simulator:
  1. PPO best checkpoint vs `nearest_job` (ratio − 1), per group and the score;
  2. PPO best checkpoint vs `rl2_gen_s_b` (ratio − 1), per group and the score;
  3. reference rows quoted from rl2.md §8.8: `rl2_gen_s_b` vs `nearest_job`
     (−6.19 / −2.86 / −2.73 / −3.51 %, score −3.82) and its teacher
     (−6.44 / −2.35 / −3.51 / −4.07 %).
  Intervals: 95 % = 1.96 × sd / √n over the realizations of a group. Reported per
  training seed, and as the mean over the three seeds: per realization the mean of
  the three ratios, then mean and interval over the realizations.
* The same realizations select the checkpoint and evaluate it, for PPO as for expert
  iteration (whose iteration 1 was chosen on them). The selection bias is the same
  for both methods and is small (10 candidates); the reserved seeds 7 000–7 029 and
  9 000–9 059 stay untouched for the owner.

### 1.6 Cost accounting

For every PPO run, from `models/<tag>_training.json`:

* wall-clock of the run, split into generation (`gen_s`), updates (`train_s`) and
  validation (`val_s`), all summed over the updates;
* CPU-seconds of generation = Σ over episodes of the worker's measured time
  (`gen_cpu_s`; one thread per worker); CPU-seconds of validation likewise
  (`val_cpu_s`); plus the upper bound wall × 40 workers;
* episodes simulated = 40 000 training + validation episodes (500 per validation,
  `nearest_job` once); decisions.

For the expert-iteration chain, from `models/{rl2_gen_s_big,rl2_gen_s_sf,rl2_gen_s_d,
rl2_gen_s_b}_training.json`: `gen_s`, `train_s`, `val_s` per iteration (the teacher's
look-aheads are inside `gen_s`), the reference validations, 47 workers; the number
of simulated episodes = acting episodes + look-ahead clones (K ≤ 6 candidates × 8
futures per labelled decision for the sampled-future teacher, K ≤ 6 for the
known-parts teacher of `rl2_gen_s_big`; counted on a sample of episodes with the
same teacher settings, seeds 190 000+, and scaled by the logged decisions) +
validation episodes (network, teacher and `nearest_job` per iteration). The
per-decision latency of a PPO checkpoint is measured once as in rl2.md §4 (single
state, one thread) to confirm it equals the network's.

### 1.7 What is not done

No hyperparameter search, no reward shaping, no KL penalty towards the frozen
network, no discounting, no resume (a crashed run is rerun from the start). The
protocol above is the baseline as the owner specified it (2026-11-23).

### 3.5 Anomaly: seed 1, greedy vs sampled decoding (`experiments/rl2_ppo_anomaly.py`)

Seed 1's greedy validation score is best at 16 000 episodes and worsens afterwards
while its sampled training cost keeps falling (§3.1). The checkpoints at 16 000 and
40 000 episodes, the last checkpoint of seed 0 and the frozen network were scored

* **The objective PPO optimizes did improve on seed 1.** The sampled policy goes
  from +47.9 % (16 000) to +26.4 % (40 000) above `nearest_job` on the validation
  realizations, consistent with its falling training cost; the greedy mode of the
  same policy goes from +7.9 % to +11.6 %. The anomaly is a divergence between
  the distribution and its mode, not a training failure.
* **The PPO policies are still far from deterministic at 40 000 episodes** (entropy

  favorable decoding for PPO, and the conclusion of §3.2 holds under either.

  specified, §1.4) picks the best mode of a still-stochastic policy, which on seed 1
  was not the last one. The protocol is kept as frozen; a longer budget or a lower
  entropy coefficient are the obvious variants (§1.7, not done).

## 4. CHANGELOG entry (integrated in `docs/CHANGELOG.md` as 0.7.29)

## 0.7.29 — 2026-11-23 — PPO baseline on the token policy (`docs/methods/rl2_ppo.md`)

* **Why.** Paper 1 claims that the search teacher is what makes the amortized
  policy good; the only reinforcement-learning comparison so far was the old PPO of
  0.5–0.6 on another representation. A PPO baseline with the **same** tokens,
  network (`DispatchNet` size S, 0.88 M parameters), action mask, simulator, cell
  distribution and validation realizations as the expert-iteration chain answers
  "what does the teacher add over the reward alone?" at equal inference cost. The
  protocol (reward, advantages, hyperparameters, budget, seeds, checkpoint
  selection, evaluation, cost accounting) was frozen and committed before the full
  runs and not changed afterwards (rl2_ppo.md §1).
* **What.** `src/rl2/ppo.py` (semi-MDP PPO between network decisions: reward = minus
  the increase of the penalized objective of `cost_so_far()`, γ = 1, GAE 0.95 in cost
  units, per-batch advantage normalization, value targets on the running scale,
  clipped surrogate 0.2, 4 epochs, minibatch 256, entropy 0.01, AdamW 3 × 10⁻⁴;
  workers return the sampled trajectories, the GPU updates; one update per 1 000
  episodes, checkpoint every 4 000 scored greedy on the validation groups, best
  kept), `experiments/rl2_ppo.py` (CLI), `experiments/rl2_ppo_eval.py` (paired
  evaluation), `experiments/rl2_ppo_cost.py` (cost of the PPO runs and of the
  expert-iteration chain, teacher look-aheads measured on a sample),
  `experiments/rl2_ppo_anomaly.py` (greedy vs sampled decoding),
  `experiments/rl2_ppo_latency.py`; `tests/test_rl2_ppo.py` (7 tests: masking,
  GAE identities, normalization, loss at ratio 1, update direction, tiny end-to-end
  run). Models `models/rl2_ppo_s0.pt`, `_s1`, `_s2` (from scratch, 40 000 episodes
  each, training seeds 200 000+ / 300 000+ / 400 000+) and `models/rl2_ppo_ft_s3.pt`,
  `_s4`, `_s5` (fine-tuning of `rl2_gen_s_b`, 12 000 episodes), with their
  `_training.json`. Results in `results/experiments/rl2_ppo_*` (not versioned),
  tables in rl2_ppo.md §3.
* **Result.** From scratch, with a comparable number of acting episodes to the
  chain (40 000 vs 36 000), PPO stays above `nearest_job` on every seed: best greedy
  scores +4.49 / +7.88 / +3.62 % (mean over seeds +5.33 %), against −3.82 % for the
  frozen expert-iteration network on the same realizations; vs that network PPO is
  +8.78 / +12.31 / +7.89 % worse (mean +9.66 %), losing on every group. Two of three
  runs were still improving at the end of the budget (≈ 0.5–1 % per 4 000
  episodes); seed 1's greedy score worsened after 16 000 episodes while its sampled
  policy kept improving (§3.5: the PPO policies are still stochastic, 0.8–1.0 nats,
  and their sampled score is 15–40 points worse than greedy). Fine-tuning the frozen
  network with PPO (V2) makes it worse on all three seeds (−2.26 / −3.22 / −2.73 %
  at the best checkpoint, the first; +1.20 % vs the frozen network on average), the
  first update moving the policy far (KL 0.06–0.15 per update). Cost: one PPO run
  = 22 min wall / 14.5 CPU-h upper bound on 40 workers, the chain = 12.7 h /
  596 CPU-h on 47 workers with ≈ 39 M look-ahead clone runs; the PPO checkpoint has
  the network's latency (1.06 ms per decision, one thread, same machine as the
  untrained reference).
* **Where.** `docs/methods/rl2_ppo.md` (§1 frozen protocol, §2 smoke test, §3
  results: learning curves, paired evaluation, cost, latency, anomaly, V2),
  `src/rl2/ppo.py`, `experiments/rl2_ppo*.py`, `tests/test_rl2_ppo.py`,
  `models/rl2_ppo_*`. Not done (rl2_ppo.md §1.7): hyperparameter search, reward
  shaping, KL penalty towards the frozen network, longer budgets.
